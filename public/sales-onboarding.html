Part 1 · The business
01 · Welcome

Welcome to ChargeGuard, Simon

ChargeGuard helps small and midsize US trucking carriers get paid for the time and money they lose at shippers and receivers: detention, layover, TONU (truck ordered, not used) and lumper charges. It records what happened at each stop, applies the terms the carrier actually agreed with that broker, works out what is owed, and prepares the claim paperwork.

As Director of Sales, your job is to bring ChargeGuard to real carriers, run discovery and demos, and turn qualified fleets into trial users and then paying customers. Your job right now also includes something less common: finding out, from real conversations, who actually buys this and why.

That matters because of where the company is. The software is built, tested and running in production. What we don't have yet is market proof. There are no paying customers, and no real carrier has yet run a claim all the way through the product. So at this stage your conversations produce two things, and both matter: revenue, and evidence. Which pain is strongest, which objections keep coming up, whether the price feels right, and what the product is missing. What you hear will directly shape what gets built next.

03 · Market

The market problem

Carriers are paid to move freight, not to wait. When a shipper or receiver keeps a truck longer than agreed, cancels a load after the truck was dispatched, or needs paid dock labor, the carrier is often entitled to extra pay: an accessorial charge. These charges are real money that is frequently earned and never collected.

Detention

Pay for waiting at a shipper or receiver beyond the agreed free time, usually about 2 hours. It's normally billed by the hour after free time ends.

Layover

Pay when a driver is held overnight or for an extended period, usually a flat fee per day instead of hourly detention.

TONU (Truck Ordered, Not Used)

A flat fee when a load is cancelled after the truck was already dispatched, which pays the carrier for the wasted trip and time.

Lumper

Reimbursement for third-party dock workers ("lumpers") the driver had to pay to load or unload. The carrier wants that money back, and the broker's terms decide how much.

How a carrier becomes entitled to these charges

Entitlement comes from the agreement for that load, usually the broker's rate confirmation and sometimes a master broker-carrier agreement. These documents set free time, hourly detention rate, layover and TONU amounts, lumper rules, and often procedural conditions such as notifying the broker while the driver is still waiting, or submitting within a deadline with specific paperwork. If the terms don't provide for a charge, or the carrier misses a condition, the claim may not be payable.

Why the money goes uncollected

Its primary source was never verified.

  • Nobody captures it in the moment. Times live in a driver's memory, a text message, a BOL scribble or ELD logs that nobody connects to billing.
  • The terms are different for every broker. Free time, rates and lumper rules vary. Looking them up for each load takes time.
  • The paperwork is a separate job. Calculating, writing up, attaching proof and sending claims is admin work that competes with dispatching trucks.
  • Follow-up is where it dies. Brokers push back, ask for more proof or go quiet. Without a system, claims are forgotten.
  • Small amounts feel not worth fighting for one at a time, but they add up across a fleet and a year.

Why small carriers are especially exposed

Large carriers have billing teams and enterprise systems. A 5-truck fleet usually has the owner, maybe one dispatcher, and no one whose job is accessorial billing. Margins are thin, so an uncollected $150 detention charge is profit the owner personally loses. Small carriers also have less leverage with brokers, which makes clean documentation and correct terms even more important.

06 · Product

How ChargeGuard works, end to end

There are three surfaces: the dispatcher web app (app.chargeguard.net), the driver phone app (driver.chargeguard.net, added to the home screen from the browser) and the claim package the carrier sends to the broker. Here's the real journey.

  1. Carrier signs up Live

    Owner

    Self-serve signup with company name, contact and fleet-size band. The 60-day trial starts immediately, with no card required. The fleet-size band sets which plan the account is placed on.

  2. Office users Limited

    Owner

    Signup creates one owner login. The system supports owner, dispatcher and billing roles (some financial actions such as write-offs and reopening are limited to owner/billing), but there is no self-serve screen to add more office users today. Additional logins have to be set up by the team, so route those requests to Ahmad.

  3. Adds drivers Live

    Dispatcher

    Enter name and phone, plus email if available. ChargeGuard emails the invite if there's an email. The dispatcher can also copy a ready-made message with the invite link and text it to the driver themselves. ChargeGuard does not send SMS. The driver sets a 6-digit PIN.

  4. Sets up brokers and their terms Live

    Dispatcher

    Each broker gets its own profile with one or more saved contracts. Upload or photograph the broker's agreement (a master agreement, or a rate confirmation that states the accessorial terms) and AI reads out free time, detention rate, layover fee, TONU fee and lumper policy. The dispatcher reviews and edits before saving, or types the terms in by hand. A contract counts as complete only when every term is filled in (a term can be $0 or "not reimbursable").

  5. Creates a trip, uploads the rate confirmation Live

    Dispatcher

    Create the trip, pick the broker and the saved contract whose terms apply, and assign a driver. Uploading the load's rate con (PDF or photo) auto-fills the load details: reference number, shipper and receiver stops. It does not set the payment terms. Those come from the contract selected in step 4. The driver gets a push notification if they turned notifications on.

  6. Driver records the stops Live

    Driver

    On the phone: Start trip → Mark arrived at each stop → snap evidence (lumper receipt, detention/POD proof, damage, other) → Mark departed → Stop trip. Each tap stamps the time. Dispatchers can also enter or correct times by hand, and those are labeled "Manually entered."

  7. Dispatcher clicks "Calculate charges" Live

    Dispatcher

    When the driver stops the trip it's marked completed. Nothing is calculated until the dispatcher clicks Calculate charges on the trip, reviews the charge lines, then clicks Generate claim. Calculation is blocked if the selected contract is incomplete. ChargeGuard uses the contract's saved terms: detention per stop past free time, layover for stays over 24 hours (if a layover fee is set), TONU if the load was cancelled, lumper per the reimbursement policy. Every line comes with a plain-English explanation of the math.

  8. Dispatcher reviews, adjusts, approves Live

    Dispatcher

    Nothing goes out without review. Lines can be adjusted through a recorded workflow, extra documents attached (BOL, POD, broker emails) and then the claim approved. A $0 claim is closed, not sent.

  9. Claim package is delivered Read carefully

    Dispatcher → broker

    For every customer account today: ChargeGuard emails the claim package (invoice PDF, the broker's terms cited, and the evidence files) to the dispatcher's own inbox. The dispatcher forwards it to the broker. Email attachments have a size limit (about 18 MB in total). If evidence doesn't fit, the email reports how many files were omitted and links to the app. The dispatcher checks the files there and sends any required missing evidence separately.

    Direct-to-broker sending is built, but switched on only for a controlled internal test account. It's the automated mode where ChargeGuard emails the broker, sends up to three reminders, handles bounces and gives the broker a one-click response page. It is not available to customers yet, and turning it on for an account is Ahmad's decision.

  10. Tracking, response and payment Live

    Dispatcher

    In the forward-it-yourself mode, the dispatcher updates the claim's status (sent, paid, disputed, written off, closed). Disputes can be answered with a revised claim, and earlier versions are kept, marked superseded. In the direct-send mode, broker responses (paid, dispute, partial) arrive through the response page automatically.

  11. Payment confirmed, claim closed Live

    Dispatcher / owner

    The carrier confirms the money actually arrived, or records "payment not located." A claim is closed with a stated reason: paid, dispute accepted, withdrawn, written off and so on. Every step is kept in the claim's history.

  12. Reporting Live

    Owner

    A trips report for selected months within the last three years, with month-by-month detention/layover/TONU/lumper totals, recovered vs. outstanding claim amounts and an Excel (.xlsx) export.

The single most important accuracy point in this section

Do not say "ChargeGuard sends the claim to the broker and chases them for you." Today the customer receives a ready-to-send package and forwards it themselves, then updates the status. Automated broker sending and reminders exist, but they're in controlled testing. If a prospect specifically needs automated broker follow-up, log it (that's valuable evidence) and tell them it's not generally available yet.

07 · Driver

What the driver experiences

Getting in

  • Invite by email, or a link the dispatcher texts them.
  • Opens in the phone's browser. "Add to home screen" makes it an app icon. No app store, nothing to download.
  • Logs in with phone number + 6-digit PIN. Forgot-PIN works if the driver has an email on file. Repeated wrong PINs lock the login for 15 minutes.

Using it

  • Sees only their own assigned trips. No broker rates or claim totals.
  • Big buttons: Start trip, Mark arrived, Mark departed, Stop trip. Can add an unplanned stop. Can decline a trip before leaving the shipper.
  • While at a stop: "Upload evidence photo," then pick a category (lumper receipt, detention/POD proof, damage, other) and take the photo with the phone camera.
  • Web Push notifications (if the driver allows them) for new trips and changes.
The "two taps" idea, stated accurately

The marketing shorthand is "two taps": Mark arrived and Mark departed at each stop. That's the core of the driver's job. Including Start and Stop trip and an optional photo, a typical one-pickup, one-delivery trip is about 6 taps across the whole day. Say "a couple of taps at each stop." Don't say "the driver only taps twice, ever."

Connectivity caveat

The driver app needs a data connection when the driver taps. It doesn't save taps offline to send later. If a dock has no signal, the tap won't go through. The driver can tap when signal returns (the time will then be later), or the dispatcher can enter the real time by hand, labeled "Manually entered." Don't claim it works offline.

Why low driver friction matters

Detention evidence is only as good as the timestamps. If the app is a hassle, drivers skip it and the claim never exists. That's why the driver sees so little: no rates, no forms, just the stop and the button.

09 · Math

How the money is calculated

ChargeGuard never applies a generic "2 hours free, $50/hour" rule. It uses the terms in the broker contract selected for the trip. If that contract is incomplete, it refuses to calculate until the dispatcher completes it, rather than guessing.

ChargeRule ChargeGuard applies
DetentionPer stop: time from arrival to departure, minus that broker's free time, times that broker's hourly rate. Calculated to the second, rounded to the cent. A rate of $0 means no detention line.
LayoverA stop longer than 24 hours bills the broker's flat layover fee, if one is set. If none is set, it becomes a "needs review" line for the dispatcher. It never silently becomes hourly detention.
TONUThe broker's flat TONU fee, applied when a trip is marked cancelled with no load.
LumperThe receipt amount (AI can read it from the photo), adjusted by the broker's policy: full, capped at an amount, a percentage, or not reimbursable.
No contract on the tripIf a trip has no contract selected, there's no rate, so no detention is calculated, and lumper receipts are billed at full face value. Always select the broker's contract.
Timestamp provenanceEvery time is labeled: Verified by device (driver tap), Manually entered (typed by office) or Source not recorded (older data). This lets the carrier, and the broker, see how strong the evidence is.

What the calculation does not check

ChargeGuard calculates from actual arrival and departure. It does not automatically check conditions like whether the driver arrived on time for the appointment, whether the broker was notified during the wait, or submission deadlines. Many brokers require these. The dispatcher's review step is where they get checked. Say "calculates the eligible amount from the saved terms," not "guarantees the claim is valid."

10 · Strategy

Why rate confirmations and contract terms matter

This is the heart of the positioning. A detention timer tells you how long the truck waited. It doesn't tell you what you're owed. The answer depends on this broker's agreement.

Free time

One broker allows 2 hours, another 3, another different times at pickup and delivery. That decides whether there's a charge at all.

Rate

$40, $50, $75 an hour, sometimes capped. A generic rate under-bills or over-bills, and over-billing invites disputes.

Notice and paperwork rules

Many agreements require notice during the wait, signed in/out times or submission within a deadline. ChargeGuard can't verify these automatically (see below).

Layover

Flat fee per day vs. nothing at all. ChargeGuard bills the fee if saved, or flags for review.

TONU

Some agreements pay a flat TONU, others nothing or conditional amounts.

Lumper

Full, capped, percentage or not reimbursable. It changes how much of the receipt the carrier gets back.

ChargeGuard's contract-aware approach

Each broker has its own saved terms. AI reads them from the agreement or rate con the carrier uploads to that broker's profile, the dispatcher confirms them, and the original document is kept. From then on every claim for that broker uses those numbers, and the claim package cites them. The result is a claim that says "here is what you agreed to," which is harder to dismiss than a generic invoice.

What ChargeGuard cannot override

It can't change the underlying agreement, create entitlement the agreement doesn't give, make up missing evidence or turn an invalid claim into a valid one. It can't stop a broker from enforcing their own contractual conditions. If the terms say no detention, ChargeGuard says no detention.

On AI reading documents

Extraction can misread a messy or unusual document. That's why extracted terms are shown for review and are editable before they're used, and why the source document is stored with the terms. Position AI as "does the typing for you, you confirm it," never "you don't need to check."

  • Enterprise TMS (e.g. McLeod, Trimble TMW): configurable accessorial modules, aimed at larger carriers. Rarely what a 10-truck fleet runs.
  • Small-carrier TMS and TMS add-ons (e.g. detention-pay add-on modules from TMS vendors): may already be in the prospect's stack. Ask what it actually does for detention before positioning against it.
  • Driver-side detention apps (time trackers with alerts): timing only.
  • Factoring companies: some help collect accessorials as part of factoring. Ask, because it changes the pain.
  • Back-office and dispatch services: a person doing it for a fee.

The biggest competitor: doing nothing

Most prospects aren't using any of the above. They're eating the loss, or chasing the occasional big one by hand. You're not displacing a vendor. You're changing a habit. That's why the most useful questions are about how many claims they don't send, and the strongest proof is a free trial on their own loads.

What not to say about competitors

Never say a competitor "doesn't work," "is a scam," "won't last," or quote their pricing or customer numbers as current fact. Never claim we recover more than they do, because we have no recovery data. If asked "how are you different from X," describe what ChargeGuard does and let the prospect compare. "I'd want you to compare them on your own loads" is a strong, honest answer.

20 · Demo

Sales demo flow

Tell one story about one load. Aim for 15 minutes, outcomes first. Use a demo account with realistic sample data, never another customer's data.

  1. The problem (2 min)

    Restate their own numbers from discovery: "You said trucks wait at X twice a week and maybe one in three gets billed."

  2. The broker's terms (2 min)

    On a broker profile, upload a sample agreement or rate con with accessorial terms. Show the terms being read out: free time, rate, layover, TONU, lumper. Show they're editable. "Does the typing, you confirm." Then create a trip, upload its rate con to fill the load details, and select that contract.

  3. The driver event (2 min)

    Show the driver app on a phone: Start trip, Mark arrived, upload a lumper receipt photo, Mark departed. Emphasize how little the driver sees.

  4. The evidence (1 min)

    Back in the office app: times labeled "Verified by device," photos attached to the stop.

  5. The calculation (2 min)

    Switch to a completed sample trip prepared before the call (realistic sample times, since live taps a minute apart won't produce detention). Click Calculate charges, then read the explanation line aloud: "4h 45m at the dock, minus 2h free time, 2h 45m at $60 = $165." Show lumper capped by policy, then click Generate claim. Say plainly that this trip uses prepared sample times.

  6. Review and approve (1 min)

    Show that nothing goes out without review. Approve.

  7. The package to send (2 min)

    Show the claim package email and PDF: evidence attached, terms cited. Be explicit: "This comes to your inbox, and you forward it to the broker."

  8. Tracking to paid (2 min)

    Mark sent, then paid. Show the claim history and the report with recovered vs. outstanding. "One list, every claim, until the money's in."

  9. Close (1 min)

    "Want to run your next few loads through it? 60 days free, no card." Offer to set up their first broker together.

Show

The broker terms being read. The driver's two key taps. The math explanation line. The finished package. The status moving to paid.

Don't spend time on

Account settings, billing screens, role permissions, every menu item, AI or technology talk, the direct-to-broker sending screens (not available to them), or internal architecture.

That we integrate with their TMS. We don't.

"Fair concern. It's not an app-store download. It opens from a link and sits on the home screen. They see only their trips and tap Mark arrived and Mark departed at each stop. No rates, no forms. And if a driver forgets, your office can enter the time by hand."

Be honest

The driver taps are what make the evidence strong. If drivers truly won't tap, the claims depend on office-entered times.

"We already track detention."

"Great. How much of what you track actually gets billed and paid? Tracking is step one. ChargeGuard is about the rest: applying each broker's terms, building the claim with evidence, and following it to paid."

"A lot don't, and that's real. Industry research says under half of detention invoices get paid. That ChargeGuard claims get paid more often. We have no data.

"$59 is too much."

Offer a discount. No discount authority exists. Log the price reaction instead, because it's important evidence.

"We use our ELD."

"Your ELD logs hours and location, which is great. Does it tell you what this broker owes you, build the claim and track payment? ChargeGuard doesn't need your ELD and doesn't replace it. It handles the billing side."

Don't say

That we pull ELD data. We don't.

"I don't want another system."

"Totally fair. Most of the work is a few taps by the driver and a review by you. No migration, no integration, nothing to replace, and month to month if it's not worth it. What would it need to do for you to think it's worth trying?"

"I don't trust AI reading my contracts."

"Good instinct. The AI just does the typing. It shows you the free time, rate and fees it found, and you check and correct them before they're used. The original document stays attached. You can also type the terms in yourself."

"We don't want software sending things to brokers."

22 · Boundaries

  • It does not create contractual entitlement where none exists.

  • It is not a generic invoicing or accounting tool.

27 · Glossary

Glossary

Accessorial
Any charge on top of the base freight rate, such as detention, layover, TONU or lumper.
Detention
Pay for waiting at a shipper or receiver past free time, usually hourly.
Free time
The waiting time allowed at a stop before detention starts, often 2 hours. Set by the agreement.
Dwell
Total time a truck spends at a facility, from arrival to departure.
Layover
Pay when a driver is held overnight or a day or more, usually a flat fee.
TONU
"Truck Ordered, Not Used." A fee when a load is cancelled after the truck was dispatched.
Lumper
Third-party dock worker paid to load or unload. The lumper fee is often paid by the driver and reimbursed.
Rate confirmation (rate con)
The broker's document confirming a load's rate and terms, often including detention, TONU and lumper terms.
Broker-carrier agreement
A master contract between a broker and a carrier that can also set accessorial terms.
BOL
Bill of Lading. The shipping document for the freight, often signed with in/out times.
POD
Proof of Delivery. The signed document showing the load was delivered.
Broker
A company that arranges freight between shippers and carriers, and usually pays the carrier.
Carrier
The trucking company that hauls the freight. ChargeGuard's customer.
Owner-operator
A driver who owns their truck and business, sometimes with a few more trucks.
Shipper
The business sending the freight. The pickup location.
Receiver / consignee
The business receiving the freight. The delivery location.
Dispatcher
The person assigning loads to drivers and managing trips, often the owner in small fleets.
Reefer
Refrigerated trailer or freight.
Dry van
Standard enclosed trailer, the most common truckload equipment.
Spot freight
One-off loads booked at current market rates, often through load boards and brokers.
Contract freight
Recurring freight at pre-agreed rates over a period.
Truckload (TL / FTL)
A shipment that fills or dedicates a whole trailer. ChargeGuard's focus.
Appointment
The scheduled pickup or delivery time. Being late can void detention under many agreements.
Short-pay
When a broker pays less than invoiced.
Factoring
Selling invoices to a factoring company for quick cash. Common with small carriers.
TMS
Transportation Management System. Software for dispatch, loads and billing.
ELD
Electronic Logging Device. A legally required device recording drivers' hours of service.
Claim
In ChargeGuard, the request for payment of accessorial charges for a trip, with its evidence.
Claim package
ChargeGuard's ready-to-send bundle: invoice PDF, evidence, broker terms cited.
PWA
Progressive Web App. A website that installs to the phone home screen like an app. That's how the driver app works.
