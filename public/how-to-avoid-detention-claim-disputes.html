How to Avoid Detention and Accessorial Claim Disputes

Most disputes over detention and accessorial claims come down to two critical issues: imprecise evidence and applying the wrong terms. ChargeGuard helps small-to-midsize trucking carriers automate the capture of accurate timestamps and apply each broker or shipper’s actual terms to minimize disputes and maximize claim recovery.

Cross-Check Before You Submit

Verifying the timestamps and the specific agreed terms against the actual rate confirmation, before a claim goes out, is what separates a claim that gets paid from one that gets disputed.

What Actually Causes a Dispute

Unverified Timestamps

Disputes often arise when timestamps are estimated or remembered rather than logged. Without precise, verifiable arrival and departure times, brokers or shippers may challenge the accuracy of the claim, leading to delays or denials.

Generic Assumptions

Applying a generic free-time or rate instead of the specific terms agreed with that broker or shipper for that load can trigger disputes. Each relationship has unique terms, and assuming standard rates without verifying them can result in incorrect claims.

Building a Defensible Claim

Practical Steps for a Strong Claim

To build a defensible claim with ChargeGuard, start by ensuring all timestamps are accurate and system-captured. Use the specific rate confirmation or contract terms for that customer as your reference point. Attach any supporting evidence, such as ELD logs or geofence data, and submit your claim promptly. This approach aligns with ChargeGuard's mechanism of applying each broker/shipper's actual terms to generate a documented claim.

Common Questions

What is the most common reason a detention claim gets disputed?

Applying a generic assumption instead of the actual agreed free-time and rate terms for that specific broker or shipper.

Does timestamp accuracy really matter for disputes?

Yes. System-captured or otherwise verifiable timestamps are much harder to dispute than manually estimated times.

Should a claim always reference the specific rate confirmation?

Yes -- citing the specific agreed terms for that load makes a claim far more defensible than a generic calculation.
