About ChargeGuard

ChargeGuard is a product of Qiyadon, built to help small-to-midsize US trucking carriers make sure every dollar a load earns actually gets identified, billed, and tracked through to payment.

Small carriers don't have a billing department. The owner or one dispatcher is doing dispatch, driver coordination, invoicing, and chasing payment -- often after hours, often from memory. Money leaks in the gaps: a rate confirmation's terms nobody re-reads at invoice time, detention or a lumper receipt that never makes it onto the bill, a short pay nobody notices because nobody's watching for it.

ChargeGuard exists to close that gap. It reads the rate confirmation so the terms are never forgotten, captures what actually happened at each stop, builds the complete invoice -- freight and accessorials together -- and tracks it through to payment, flagging exactly what's still missing. For a carrier without a back-office team dedicated to this, that's the difference between money quietly left on the table and money that's actually identified, billed, and followed all the way to payment.

Built for the Fleet That Doesn't Have a Back Office for This

Owner-operators and small-to-midsize fleets rarely have a dedicated team tracking revenue across every load and broker. ChargeGuard exists to close that gap automatically, so getting paid what you earned doesn't depend on someone remembering to chase it down.

Why This Starts With Detention and Accessorials

ChargeGuard started by solving detention and accessorial recovery specifically, because it's one of the clearest, most measurable places revenue leaks for small carriers. In 2023, detention affected 39.3% of all truckload stops, costing the industry an estimated $15.1 billion -- $3.6 billion in direct expenses and $11.5 billion in lost productivity, according to the American Transportation Research Institute (ATRI) 2024 driver detention study. Despite that, 94.5% of fleets charge detention fees, but fewer than 50% of those invoices actually get paid.

That's still core to ChargeGuard today -- but it's one piece of a broader job: making sure the whole load, not just the accessorials, gets correctly identified, billed, and accounted for from the rate confirmation through to reconciled payment.
