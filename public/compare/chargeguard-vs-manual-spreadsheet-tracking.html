ChargeGuard vs. Spreadsheets for Load Revenue Assurance

A spreadsheet can hold the same information ChargeGuard tracks -- rate confirmation terms, accessorial charges, invoice status, payments. The difference is what it takes to keep it accurate as your fleet grows. Here's how the two compare, stage by stage.

Published 2026-09-02, updated 2026-11-02

Reading the Rate Confirmation

ChargeGuard reads the rate confirmation itself -- contracted linehaul, fuel surcharge, mileage, and the specific accessorial terms for that broker or shipper -- so the terms are captured once and don't depend on someone re-reading the document correctly every time. A spreadsheet requires someone to manually re-enter those terms for every load, which is slow and where terms most often get missed or mistyped.

Tracking Contracted and Earned Revenue

ChargeGuard keeps what a load was contracted to pay and what it actually earned -- including detention, layover, TONU, or lumper reimbursement backed by captured evidence -- as one connected record. In a spreadsheet, contracted revenue and the extra charges tend to live in separate tabs or separate tools, which makes it easy for a legitimate accessorial charge to go unbilled.

Building the Invoice

ChargeGuard combines contracted freight revenue and every legitimate accessorial charge into one documented invoice or billing packet, with evidence attached and tax applied where applicable. A spreadsheet can list the same charges, but assembling them into an actual invoice -- with the right terms cited and evidence attached -- is still a manual step for every load.

Tracking Payment and Short Pays

ChargeGuard records payments by hand or from a bank/factoring CSV, matches them to invoices automatically where it can, and opens a tracked recovery case the moment a broker pays less than the invoice. In a spreadsheet, a short pay is just a number that doesn't match -- easy to miss in a long row of entries, and easy to forget to follow up on.

Keeping Up as the Fleet Grows

ChargeGuard's structure stays the same whether you're tracking 5 loads a week or 500 -- the record for each load is built the same way. Spreadsheets tend to work fine at a small scale and become genuinely unwieldy as rows, tabs, and formulas multiply with fleet size.

Cost

ChargeGuard is a flat-rate monthly subscription by fleet size, with no per-load or per-claim cost. A spreadsheet is free to open, but the time spent keeping it accurate and chasing down what's missing is a real cost most carriers don't track.
