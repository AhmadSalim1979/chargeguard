What Founder-Led SMBs Should Know About 'Should'

The word 'should' is a modal verb used to express obligation, advice, or expectation. It's often confused with 'shall,' which has a more formal and binding connotation.

Published 2026-09-06

What Does 'Should' Mean?

'Should' is used to indicate what is considered the right or best thing to do. It suggests a recommendation or expectation rather than a strict rule.

For example, 'You should eat more vegetables' implies that eating vegetables is a good idea, but it's not a command.

'Should' vs. 'Shall'

'Should' is often used in informal or advisory contexts, while 'shall' is more formal and is typically used in legal or formal documents to indicate obligation.

For instance, 'You should arrive by 9 AM' is a suggestion, whereas 'You shall arrive by 9 AM' is a directive.

Common Uses of 'Should'

'Should' is frequently used to give advice, such as 'You should study for the exam.'

It can also express an expectation, like 'The package should arrive by Friday.'

Additionally, 'should' can indicate a probable situation, for example, 'It should be sunny tomorrow.'

Grammatical Rules for 'Should'

'Should' is followed by the base form of a verb. For example, 'You should go to the doctor.'

It is used with all subjects, whether singular or plural, and does not change form.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is 'should' the same as 'must'?

No, 'should' is less strict than 'must.' 'Must' indicates a strong obligation or necessity, while 'should' is more of a recommendation.

Can 'should' be used in questions?

Yes, 'should' can be used in questions to ask for advice or opinion, such as 'Should I take this job offer?'
